Van Vleck perturbation theory "folds down" the Hilbert space onto the few states we care about and gives an effective Hamiltonian that still remembers the far-away states. Read the first two notes in this order; the other three take up separate questions and can be read in any order after them.

1. [[Van Vleck perturbation theory - near and far|Near and far]] — Why we fold down, the comparison that decides which states to keep, and what a unitary rotation does to states and operators.
2. [[Van Vleck perturbation theory and the effective Hamiltonian|The effective Hamiltonian]] — The derivation, from $H=H_0+\lambda V$ to the effective Hamiltonian at third order.
3. [[Van Vleck perturbation theory - dressed states and rotated operators|Dressed states and rotated operators]] — What the model states stand for, and why other operators must be rotated too.
4. [[Van Vleck perturbation theory - errors|Errors]] — How large the errors of a truncated effective Hamiltonian are, and when the folding fails.
5. [[Van Vleck perturbation theory - symmetries|Symmetries]] — Why the effective Hamiltonian inherits the symmetries of the full one.
